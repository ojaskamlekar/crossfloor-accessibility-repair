\title{Exact Topology-Preserving Cross-Floor Accessibility Repair with Shared Physical Assets}
\author{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Restoring access across existing floors requires optimizing complete interventions while preserving room topology and physical asset semantics. We formalize strict cross-floor repair with frozen room polygons and adjacency, profile-specific rooted demands, atomic costs and verified shared-asset reuse. Rectangular footprints are compiled into owned collision cells. Two exact sweeps apply established representative-partition machinery to profile route witnesses while retaining every paid footprint. The complete-unit sweep retains owners within coupling units; an alternative cell-local sweep expires an owner name only after its last possible reuse at that cell. Its bound depends on individual action spans and owner-lifetime overlap rather than an entire shared shaft's span. We give correctness arguments, explicit preprocessing and label accounting, and a two-floor physical-bundle hardness boundary. Independent CP-SAT flow optimization, improved whole-action greedy, exhaustive small checks and replayable solutions support evaluation. A pinned campaign records 1,317 measurements across 69 experiment/case pairs; a separate 207-measurement checkpoint tests the local sweep. On declared 16- and 32-floor chains, earlier DP modes hit a state cap while local expiration and CP-SAT complete. A four-storey, eight-room IFC-derived control changes its optimal shaft chain when horizontal profile permissions change; local expiration preserves its cost and reduces peak raw states. These are controlled subsets with hypothetical access annotations. The contribution is an inspectable physical/interface transfer with action-span and owner-lifetime accounting. Geometric guarantees concern supplied two-dimensional templates, not construction safety or statutory compliance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Building information models distinguish physical space, openings and connectivity \cite{bim,ava}; automated compliance checking also requires explicit, computable requirements \cite{amor}. A repair decision requires more than finding a missing graph edge. A shared wall need not admit traversal; an existing connector need not have permission for every accessibility profile. A proposed installation can affect multiple floors and destinations for one price, overlap another installation, or share a physical facility with a compatible action. These distinctions matter when room topology is frozen rather than regenerated. Here topology-preserving means that room identities, polygons and the shared-boundary adjacency graph remain exactly equal to their input values. Traversable profile access is a separate graph and may change. Candidates are supplied fixed templates, not automatically inferred construction proposals; exactness refers to the least feasible original cost/count/identifier label in that finite model.

Consider Fig.~\ref{fig:demo}: two profiles need to reach two upstairs destinations. A baseline lift reaches circulation room C for both profiles, while an existing door connects C to T0 for only profile zero. Only one of four obligations initially holds. One original action purchases two new lift effects for three abstract cost units, enabling every obligation without changing room adjacency. Separate effects cost four. A graph-only cheapest repair need not be physically compatible, and independently pricing effects changes the optimization problem.

\subsection{A Complete Accounting Example}
Figure~\ref{fig:complete-diagnostic} makes the difficulty explicit. Three floors each contain rooms L and R. Baseline doors admit both profiles except the middle door, which admits only profile zero. Each profile must reach L1 and R2 from L0. Original action A costs one and supplies disconnected edges a=L0--L1 and u=R1--R2. B costs one and supplies b=L1--L2 using the same left asset as a. C costs one half and supplies R0--R1 through a different owner overlapping u's paid middle-floor footprint; D is a disjoint cost-three escape on R0--R1.

A purchase vertex connected to all endpoints of A falsely reports cost one: it invents a profile-one route from L0 to R2. Correct optimization selects A+B for cost two. A's edge u may be omitted from the profile-one witness L0--L1--L2--R2, yet its complete paid footprint stays occupied and excludes C. Before B, the live state retains left and right owner identities, the mandatory R2 block, the anchored L0/L1 block, and label (1,1,(A)). B reuses the left asset consistently. This example has hand-derived optimum two, a direct false-shortcut check and an independent rectangle-occupancy check.

\subsection{Research Question and Contributions}
We ask: \emph{Can exact rooted accessibility repair preserve complete physical actions and shared ownership while keeping exponential state dependence on a measured floor interface rather than raw catalogue identities?} The scope is precise: independent physical portal activations, rectangular fixed templates, nonnegative costs, separate rooted profile layers, and a reserved-interface two-dimensional kernel.

The paper develops three connected contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An owned-cell representation preserving complete physical purchases, verified same-owner reuse and original cost/count/identifier ordering, with complete-unit and cell-local last-use sweeps.
\item A full-action/thin-witness correctness argument, safe component pruning and transfer of established weighted representative partitions to the measured interface, yielding \eqref{eq:bound} and the action-span bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:local-reuse}; a physical two-floor hardness boundary explains why floor count alone is insufficient.
\item A manifest-driven evaluation with an independent CP-SAT flow optimizer and whole-action greedy heuristic, alongside exhaustive small-instance checks, analytical scaling and licensed building-derived controlled inputs.
\end{enumerate}
These are scoped method and evaluation contributions. Shared-cost labelled connectivity and rank-based partition reduction are prior work. A difference from one concrete generic upper bound does not establish first-ever priority or a lower bound on every existing method.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\subsection{Architectural Access and Labelled Network Design}
BIM/IFC reasoning already distinguishes adjacency, openings and accessible routes \cite{bim}. Our contract uses that distinction; it does not claim its invention. Minimum labelled connectivity attaches cost to labels shared by edges \cite{labels}; profile-separated bundled activation therefore has an existing network-design interpretation. Heterogeneous graph connectivity makes condition-dependent access explicit \cite{condition}. Simultaneous network design also optimizes infrastructure usable by several restricted link classes \cite{simultaneous}. Profiles or bundles alone do not establish originality.

Architectural generation and geometric repair have different decision contracts. EPSAP evolves multi-level room/opening and vertical-facility geometry with evolutionary and local-search operators \cite{epsap}. PlanFix projects mutable room geometry to reduce violation/displacement energy; its inspected preprint distinguishes energy convergence from feasibility/global optimality and excludes global accessibility routing from the current projector scope \cite{planfix}. Those methods motivate a geometric intervention problem but are not common-class runtime competitors for frozen-polygon, original-action subset optimization. Table~\ref{tab:related} states these scoped correspondences; it does not establish absence of an equivalent prior algorithm.


\subsection{Graph Repair and Multi-Floor Generation}
Al-Harasis and Jabi's benchmark-guided method is the closest architectural graph-repair comparator \cite{graphrepair}. It extracts door-connected IFC spaces, selects programme benchmarks by space-type similarity, adjusts areas and edges in an evaluation graph, and optionally inserts missing programme nodes. Three projects are evaluated with a GraphConv score. The authors explicitly separate these graph changes from IFC modification; synthetic completion does not establish geometric realization. The inspected method does not specify minimum-cost physical action selection or an optimality theorem. Our distinction is therefore the decision and verification contract, not the word ``repair.''

MANSION generates building-scale scenes with vertical cores and room partitions; its task-editing stage places or removes scene objects \cite{mansion}. Multi-floor navigability is relevant prior capability, but generation and scene editing do not establish minimum-cost portal installation over independently frozen input floors. Table~\ref{tab:related} compares contracts; ``NE'' means not established by the inspected formulation, rather than an impossibility claim.

The Revit/Dynamo/AVA workflow of Bayraktar Sari and Jabi computes spatial graphs and analyses, including vertical circulation \cite{ava}. Existing cross-level analysis is consequently not our research gap. EPSAP and PlanFix also demonstrate why maintaining an input layout needs a precise definition: geometric similarity or displacement penalties are weaker than equality of the input room polygons and adjacency.


Conflict-aware Steiner selection and conflict-free coverage study restricted support and incompatibility structures \cite{conflicts,conflictcover}. Minimum-label parameterized complexity already supplies hardness boundaries for shared edge labels \cite{minlabel}. Physical locality alone does not imply a small floor interface. An atomic action with disconnected graph effects also cannot be represented faithfully by a purchase vertex adjacent to every effect endpoint: that vertex invents paths between disconnected supplied components. A correct reduction separates effect occurrences from purchase decisions and retains all paid physical occupancy.

\subsection{Connectivity Compression and the Generic Comparison}
Weighted connectivity partitions admit cut-matrix rank compression and representative families \cite{rank}. We reuse that machinery. The ownership/cell state, complete unit lifetimes, full-action accounting and physical-to-witness completion correspondence are the project transfer.

Weighted distance-neighborhood logic \cite{logic} supplies a concrete generic comparison. Its resource graph has one Usage object per owner/cell, separate Action and supplied-edge Occurrence objects, profile Vertices and Cells. A fixed radius-one, threshold-two formula enforces selected usages, cell capacity one and rooted connectivity via an artificial hub. A supplied linear clique-width expression and membership-weight objective transfer yield
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{rec}}+2^{O(prL+zL+p+zLh)}\poly(B).
\label{eq:generic}
\end{equation}
The transfer uses the source's v2 Theorem 1.2 and weighted definition, through clique-width; it is not the quadratic tree-width entry for connectivity constraints. Appendix~\ref{app:generic} gives the exact formulation. No published generic optimizer is executed in our runtime experiment.

Our displayed owner term is logarithmic rather than linear in $h$. For fixed $h$ the asymptotic interface exponents coincide. Bounded-width Steiner forest results further show that the demand structure matters when translating connectivity bounds \cite{steinerwidth}. A narrower encoding or domain-valued resource/connectivity method might match our dependence. Thus \eqref{eq:generic} compares constructed upper bounds, not a universal separation or priority proof.

\section{Strict Repair Model}
\subsection{Frozen Topology, Profiles and Actions}
An instance has ordered floors, room polygons/identifiers, shared-boundary adjacency $A$, baseline connections, profiles $q\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$, rooted obligations $(s_q,t)$, and original actions $a$. Each nonvacuous profile has one source and any number of targets; vacuous layers use a canonical harmless anchor. Within-floor doors and inter-floor portals induce profile graphs $H_q$ according to active permissions. Layers never mix: a shared price does not permit profile switching along a route.

Strict repair selects complete physical actions $S$, monotonically activating supplied connection bundles. Its objective label is
\begin{equation}
\ell(S)=\left(\sum_{a\in S}c_a,\ |S|,\ \operatorname{sort}(S)\right),
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
ordered lexicographically. Nonnegative original costs use exact decimal arithmetic on JSON numeric representations. Activation is idempotent; each selected original action is charged once. Final polygons, identifiers and $A$ equal the baseline.

Candidate doors, dependencies, declared exclusions, shape changes and metadata-only corrections are outside this solver. Existing baseline doors are permitted. Relaxed topology-changing repair is a separate problem, not mixed into our theorem/evaluation.

\subsection{Geometry and Owner Identity}
The kernel checks footprint/reservation containment, profile width and landing requirements, obstacle avoidance, aligned portal footprints and joint overlap. Room access uses a square-body clearance abstraction. Templates and reservations are supplied evidence or declared experimental assumptions, not inferred from proximity.

Distinct selected portal owners cannot share a collision cell. Same-owner overlap requires global kind, width, physical-profile and per-floor footprint consistency. A selected action occupies its own complete effect footprints, rather than unselected catalogue portions of its owner. Conversely, an irrelevant paid graph effect never loses its footprint.

This fixed two-dimensional model does not certify slab openings, headroom, structure, moving equipment, operational lifts or building-code compliance. Geometry uses the recorded GEOS version and tolerance $10^{-8}$, not exact symbolic construction predicates.

\section{Recognition and the Owned Interface}
Contract baseline within-floor access separately per floor/profile. Historical default runs retain all components. The optional marked-component preprocessing below retains only obligation or possible external-edge components, without changing any physical action or input room. Rectangular arrangements produce collision cells above the tolerance, with checked equivalence to the kernel's pairwise conflicts. Tiny arrangement cells outside that contract are conservatively unsupported.

The bipartite incidence graph of available actions and owners defines complete coupling units $J$. Shared owners join actions; collision edges between units do not merge them. Unit support contains every effect/footprint floor of every available action in the unit. Action-by-action span alone is insufficient.

\section{Algorithm and Correctness}
The pipeline is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}. Sweep floors with an $L$-floor look-ahead window and permanent virtual roots, one per profile. A state contains live cell status, selected boundary vertices and profile connectivity partitions. During a unit, a cell is empty, externally occupied or owned by an allowed owner of that unit. After the complete unit, owner labels become occupied bits. Labels retain \eqref{eq:objective} and complete original identifiers.

\subsection{Transitions}
Introduce window vertices and mark targets selected. Attach each actual source to its permanent root. At their earliest endpoint floor, baseline edges branch on witness inclusion without changing baseline materialization. At a unit's first support floor, process every original action once: skip, or take it, pay its full cost and fill all actual effect cells. Reject external/different-owner occupancy. For each taken action, choose subsets of complete supplied connected components separately by profile and glue their witness partitions.

After all unit actions, erase owners to bits. After incident unit/baseline events, forget the floor. Reject a selected non-root block when its last live vertex disappears; drop expired cells. Accept only rooted target witnesses. Replay selected original actions through geometry, adjacency and reachability before returning the minimum accepting label.

\subsection{Conditional Guarantee and State Size}
The proof has four ingredients: identity-dependent reuse stays inside a complete unit; thin witnesses preserve complete repairs; occupancy/partitions suffice for a fixed future continuation; and a minimum-label product cut-row basis preserves the best accepting completion. Appendix~\ref{app:proof} proves these statements, including label order and forgetting.

\begin{theorem}[Exact repair on recognized physical interfaces]
For rooted independent fixed portal activations with consistent owners, checked collision-cell equivalence and complete support bound $L$, the unlimited-state algorithm returns the optimum strict original-action label. Its bit-complexity bound is
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{rec}}+2^{O(prL+zL\log(h+2))}\poly(B).
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
This guarantee concerns the recognized finite model. It does not assert a lower bound on generic optimizers or verify physical construction. Inside a unit the resource factor is at most $\prod_c(h(J,c)+2)\le(h+2)^{zL}$. Between units it is $2^{zL}$. Summing representative limits over optional boundary sets gives $3^{prL}$. Witness branching and cut elimination add $2^{O(prL)}$ work; exact costs/identifiers add polynomial bit work. The exponent is an interface encoding, not the plain sum $p+r+z+L+h$.

The implementation caps raw states at 100,000 and cut columns at 65,536. Limit errors are operational failures, not infeasibility or a claim that no unexplored optimum exists.


\subsection{Structural Meaning and a Hardness Boundary}
The natural parameter is the information that survives a floor cut: profile components, occupied cells and still-live asset identity. For example, floors with a bounded number of connected circulation zones and local vertical installations can have bounded $r,z,L,h$ while room and action counts grow. This is a conditional class, not a property assumed of all buildings. A shared asset coupling many floors or a long baseline portal increases $L$; many disconnected profile components or overlapping reservations increase the interface.

\begin{lemma}[Safe marked-component preprocessing]\label{lem:pruning}
For each profile, mark baseline within-floor components containing its root, a target, an active cross-floor baseline endpoint or any available supplied-edge endpoint. Removing unmarked components from the connectivity abstraction preserves feasible selected action sets and their full objective labels. Complete footprints and source geometry are retained.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An unmarked component contains no obligation and has no possible edge to another component under any action selection. Its removal changes no required root--target path. Conversely, every retained path exists in the original graph. Cost and occupancy are unchanged because original actions remain intact.
\end{proof}
Thus the same bound uses the maximum marked-component count $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ when this option is enabled. Its implementation is checked against full-mode/oracle labels, including a case reducing $r$ from eleven to one after adding ten isolated rooms per floor. No compression speedup follows merely from this parameter reduction.

\begin{theorem}[Two-floor physical-bundle boundary]\label{thm:hardness}
Budgeted strict repair is NP-hard with two floors, one profile, unit costs, $L=2$, $h=1$, and no collisions, when an action may purchase an arbitrary finite bundle of private portals. With polynomial subset verification the represented decision problem is NP-complete.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Reduce Set Cover \cite{karp}. Put one source room below and one disjoint target room per universe element above. For each set--element incidence, reserve a distinct aligned unit rectangle on both floors and supply a private lift portal between the source and that element's target. Each set becomes one unit-cost physical action buying all its incidence portals. Rectangular target strips have width linear in their incidence counts; the source spans their union. Gaps separate strips and all portal patches are disjoint. Coordinates and catalogue size are polynomial. No baseline cross-floor edge exists; rooms and adjacency stay frozen.

A target is reachable exactly when a selected set contains its element. A repair of cost at most $k$ therefore exists exactly when a cover of size at most $k$ exists. Every owner occurs in one action, so each complete unit spans two floors and $h=1$. A selected set is checked by cost, geometry and profile reachability in polynomial time under the fixed kernel. The executable construction checks this correspondence on small subset enumerations.
\end{proof}
The result inherits classical coverage hardness and is not a new reduction technique. It rules out floor-count-only tractability unless $P=NP$, but leaves single-shaft restrictions open. Here $r$ and $z$ grow: it does not contradict \eqref{eq:bound}, prove that exponent optimal, or establish a separation from treewidth, clique-width or CP-SAT.

\section{Experimental Design}
\subsection{Questions and Comparators}
We evaluate (Q1) exact semantic agreement on small inputs; (Q2) catalogue/owner scaling under measured interfaces; (Q3) compression benefit/overhead and operational boundaries; and (Q4) an auditable building-derived controlled adaptation. Four semantic controls test why geometry, ownership and original prices cannot be simplified silently.

Compressed/uncompressed modes solve the same recognized class. The oracle enumerates every subset on at most eight actions, with certificate replay through separate union-find/re-enumeration. Geometry/schema predicates are shared. Larger dense inputs use an analytical lower bound/witness rather than exhaustive certificates. The resource-logic evaluator checks an encoding, not a published optimizer runtime. Earlier shaft/partition solvers have narrower subclasses and are not treated as common-class competitors.


\subsection{Independent Same-Model Optimizers}
The CP-SAT baseline \cite{cpsat} uses Boolean action variables $x_a$ and owner/cell usages $y_{oc}=\bigvee_{a:(o,c)\in U_a}x_a$, with $\sum_o y_{oc}\le1$ for each cell. $U_a$ includes all paid footprints. For each profile, a separate integral flow sends one unit from its root to each distinct non-root target. Baseline edges are enabled without purchase; other edges are enabled by the OR of their supplying actions. Each directed edge capacity is the number of targets times its enablement. Conservation makes this equivalent to rooted reachability. Routing uses only actual room edges, never an artificial purchase connector.

Decimal costs are scaled to exact integers within the solver's checked range. Consecutive solves fix minimum cost, minimum action count, then prefer membership of each ascending original identifier. With count fixed this minimizes the sorted identifier tuple. Every stage must return OPTIMAL; interruption is a limit with a separately replayed incumbent, never an exact optimum. OR-Tools 9.14.6206 uses one worker and seed zero. Recognition, integer scaling and replay are included in timed work; this solver shares geometry recognition but no DP connectivity machinery.

Greedy selects admissible whole actions by cost per newly reachable profile--room pair, retaining shared-owner occupancy. A dead end is heuristic failure, not infeasibility. This heuristic and the optimizer provide genuine solver comparisons. The four deliberately altered semantic controls remain separate from these baselines.

\subsection{Frozen Recipes and Measurements}
The 37 authored cases cover shared assets, partial footprints, atomic bundles, unused paid geometry, profile separation, cyclic witnesses, multiple rooms/destinations, owner multiplicities 2/3/4/8/16, dense candidate counts 8/32/64/128, seeds 0--5 crossed with (edges,profiles)=(2,1)/(4,2)/(6,3), and state-cap/dependency/tiny-cell controls. Parameters vary jointly; this is not a full factorial study isolating every parameter.

Three fresh-process samples are recorded per recognition/DP mode and CP-SAT, one greedy sample, and one oracle sample per eligible input. Wall time starts inside the worker after imports. Solver times include internal recognition and replay. Separate recognition timing is never subtracted to fabricate DP-only time. Process timeout is 60 seconds. Local timings are descriptive, not isolated hardware significance tests.

Each measurement stores seed, canonical input/source hashes, settings, clean revision, Python/packages/GEOS, machine and status. The retained core records contain 362 authored and 80 historical Duplex manifests. Corrected authored CP-SAT/greedy measurements add 148; the twelve new public-source cases add 132. Every published timing excludes the discarded import-timing pilot. Core small comparisons have 29+8 oracle certificates, plus the demo: 38. The twelve new public-source certificates bring the total to 50. Successful comparable status/cost/original-ID labels agree exactly. Unsupported/limit/timeout states are not recoded as infeasibility. Appendix~\ref{app:coverage} preserves full coverage.

\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
\subsection{Meaning of the Contribution}
The central result preserves physical/action semantics under a compact owned interface and rooted witness reduction. Semantic controls make that preservation observable. A raw-catalogue-independent exponential term is useful on the analytical families, but polynomial recognition remains substantial and measured parameters can grow. The comparison with \eqref{eq:generic} is parameter accounting rather than empirical superiority over a published implementation.

\subsection{Limits of Evidence}
Correctness evidence is finite and the formal argument still needs independent review. Certificate graph/replay logic is separate, but schema/geometry are shared, so common geometry errors remain possible. Numerical recognition can reject small cells. Arbitrary demands, candidate doors, implications/exclusions, selection-dependent obstruction and independent-background metadata corrections are excluded. Large analytical inputs lack exhaustive optimum certificates.

The historical suite has only three local timing repetitions; ratios can reflect workstation variability. The additional protocol below separately identifies its repetition policy and code revision. Compression helps little on this corpus and can fail its cut-column cap where full mode succeeds. Generators do not isolate every parameter independently. The two historical Duplex subsets and three admitted public-source subsets use hypothetical annotations and one room per selected floor. They cannot establish actual renovations, costs or full-building generality; two screened sources remain unsupported. The new hypothetical feasible reference is distinct from original-IFC restoration truth; no human accessibility study is supplied. The additional multi-room experiment below is a separate extracted-subset study with hypothetical access annotations; it does not retroactively turn historical variants into independent buildings.

The supported result is a semantics-preserving physical/interface transfer with the explicit bound in \eqref{eq:bound}. Weighted logic and the executed CP-SAT formulation can express the recognized problem. The displayed logarithmic owner term improves on one constructed linear-owner expression bound, not on every possible resource/connectivity algorithm. Literature priority for this transfer remains unconfirmed; rank bases, tensoring and shared-cost connectivity remain attributed prior methods.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented exact strict repair that keeps frozen room topology and complete original action accounting while compressing profile-specific route witnesses. Owned cells and complete units preserve reuse/collisions, and the conditional bound exposes a measured interface rather than raw identities. Controlled comparisons, explicit semantic failures and licensed building-derived experiments provide reproducible evidence with physical and operational limits intact. The representation, provenance and evidence boundaries define the scope of subsequent extensions.
